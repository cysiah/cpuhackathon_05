01IntroductionTeam 05 analytical tool

How much hotter can it get before each retrofit fails?

Brutalist Broiler gives every atrium retrofit one extra number: the degrees of further warming it can take before its overheating score passes 42 K·h.

Audience

MMU estates and the design team choosing an atrium retrofit meant to last decades.

Question

Which package stays below the overheating limit the longest, and what does that cost in daylight and openings?

Decision

Among the 1,041 designs that pass, pick one with headroom, not one that scrapes through.

03Scope of studyAdaptive reuse

From the whole stock down to one atrium

Part O’s overheating rules cover new homes only. Existing buildings, and buildings converted to a new use, go unchecked. We study one of them closely enough to act on.

05Our tool · How it works

The same sweep, repeated in a warmer world

  1. 01
    Warmer weatherShift the 2050 file by +0.5, +1 and +2 °C, the way FF Climate Morph does.
  2. 02
    Re-run EnergyPlus243 designs × 4 climates, about 1,000 runs of the official model.
  3. 03
    HeadroomThe extra warming each design takes before it passes 42 K·h, with its error measured.
  4. 04
    Show itColour the trade-off front by headroom. A “safe until” card for any design.

    10SignificanceOpenness Analysis tool

    Why openness is the thing to analyse

    1. 01
      Evaluate the existing buildingScore the atrium as built before any proposal: 320.6 K·h, with 58 of 70 occupied hours above 26 °C.
    2. 02
      Locate weak pointsFind where the heat gets in. The atrium glass is 16% of the envelope but takes 28% of the hot-week sun.
    3. 03
      Cross-reference any proposalRun any design through the same model and benchmark it against the existing building on the same score, with its error.
    4. 04
      Openness is where the factors meetSolar gain, shading, day airflow, night purge and conduction all act through how open the envelope is, and they interact: the U-value effect even changes sign with shading.

    Lines per bundle are proportional to each lever’s spread: the range of the mean score across its five levels, over all 3,125 EnergyPlus runs. Roof screen 248 K·h, g-value 193, night ventilation 133, day ventilation 118, U-value 91.

    11Our tool · Trade-offs and honesty

    Every number labelled, every error measured

    A design with lots of headroom usually needs a heavy screen or very low g-value, which costs daylight, and big night vents. We plot headroom against daylight, vent opening and winter heat loss on one front.
