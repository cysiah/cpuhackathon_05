ledger

Overheating is one line in a longer account. This guide books the atrium’s heat, energy, daylight and carbon side by side, shows what the starter pack can already answer about each, and sets out how Rhino geometry is brought into the tool as JSON.

Why overheating is a sustainability question

A building that overheats rarely stays as it is. Someone eventually installs cooling, and then the problem has moved from comfort into energy and carbon for the rest of the building’s life. The sustainable fix keeps the atrium comfortable without buying that cooling, and without throwing away the daylight, winter warmth and embodied carbon the building already has. The building stays: its concrete, frame and glass are carbon already spent, and the most sustainable option starts from them.

Every option is therefore assessed against four accounts at once.

PeopleHeat stress, sleepy afternoons, a space people avoid. The score and TM52 live here.
EnergyCooling, fans and electric light added to make up for a bad passive design.
CarbonOperational carbon from that energy, plus embodied carbon in every new screen, pane or panel.
Resilience2050 summers are hotter, and a fix that only just passes today fails first.

The cooling hierarchy

UK practice orders cooling measures from passive to active (the London Plan sets it out in policy SI 4). Each rung is used as far as it goes before the next one is considered, because each rung down costs more energy and carbon to run.

  1. Keep heat outThe sun is stopped before it becomes heat inside.
    In the sweepRoof g-value, external roof screen
    Beyond itLess glass on the south roof slope, fritted or PV glass, a reflective roof finish
  2. Store it, release itThe day’s heat is absorbed by the concrete and released at night.
    In the sweepNight ventilation (the existing 1,000 m² of exposed concrete is fixed)
    Beyond itMore exposed mass, phase-change panels
  3. Ventilate passivelyHot air is let out at the top and cooler air drawn in low down.
    In the sweepDay ventilation, as a fixed air-change rate
    Beyond itReal vent areas and heights, wind
  4. Move the airAir speed makes the same temperature feel cooler.
    In the sweepNothing
    Beyond itCeiling or large slow fans
  5. Cool mechanicallyThe last resort, and the one a sustainable design avoids.
    In the sweepNothing
    Beyond itA benchmark: the cooling energy the passive rungs save

Beyond degree-hours: TM52

The sprint score answers “how much above 26 °C”. UK designers also use CIBSE TM52, which compares each hour with an adaptive limit. People accept warmer rooms after a run of warm days, so the limit rises with the running mean outdoor temperature: limit = 0.33 × Trm + 18.8 + 3 K. A room fails if it fails two of these three criteria.

1 · How oftenHours at least 1 K over the limit: no more than 3% of occupied hours.
49 h baseline, against a limit of 2.1 h
2 · How bad on one dayDaily weighted exceedance (degrees over × hours): no more than 6.
99 on the worst day
3 · Never this hotNo hour more than 4 K over the limit.
13 K over at the peak

Of the 3,125 simulated designs, 1,041 pass the sprint score but only 241 pass TM52 over the hot week. The app computes TM52 from the hourly EnergyPlus results derived. Criterion 1 is meant for May to September, so one heatwave week makes it far stricter. The result is read as a stress test that separates comfortable passes from scraped ones, not as compliance.

Rhino to JSON: how geometry reaches the tool

The web app never reads Rhino. It reads JSON files in data/, and three small scripts write them. Each one is run from Rhino 8 (ScriptEditor → open the file → Run). All three convert model units to metres and keep Rhino coordinates; true north stays 60.9° anticlockwise from +Y.

Atrium tagging

export_atrium_geometry.py reads roles from layers. Its first run offers to create them. Exposed and Mass faces together must join into one closed volume.

LayerContentsBecomes in EnergyPlus
ATRIUM::ExposedFaces touching outside air: roof slopes, clerestories, gablesOutdoor surfaces, glazing allowed
ATRIUM::MassFloor and walls to the rest of the buildingAdiabatic thermal mass (downward faces become the floor)
ATRIUM::GlazingGlass panes lying on Exposed faces, within 0.5 mGlazed area on the face it sits on

Objects that must stay on their own layer in the shared RhinoSync model can carry a key instead: atrium_role = exposed, mass or glazing, set under Properties → Attribute User Text. A key always takes precedence over the layer.

The script writes tools/inputs/atrium_geometry.json, which the sweep uses in place of its built-in box, and updates the floor, volume and glass areas in data/baseline.json, which the app’s proxies use. One face looks like this (illustrative values):

    Conditions for a sweep

    • data/sweep.json is overwritten by every run, including --quick, so a copy is kept beforehand.
    • Group 0 and the FF components stay unedited. The export scripts only read from them.
    • Faces must be flat. Curved glass is split into planar panels; holes in faces are ignored and reported by the script.
    • The baseline card goes stale. After a new atrium export, the baseline EnergyPlus score in the app belongs to the old geometry until the Grasshopper export is re-run.
    • Run time. The full grid took about 25 minutes on 14 cores; a laptop takes well over an hour. Every new lever with five levels multiplies the run count by five.
    • Reverting. Deleting atrium_geometry.json returns the sweep to the original box.

    Questions to take into the day

    The accounts

    • Which account does the best option draw on: daylight, winter heat, energy or carbon?
    • Does a passing design scrape through the score but fail TM52?
    • Would air conditioning still be added to the proposed solution, and if not, why not?

    The hierarchy

    • Have the top rungs been used before fans or cooling are considered?
    • Which roof slope is the move aimed at, and why that one?
    • Is the night purge passive, or does it need fans? What does that cost a week?

    The model

    • Which numbers are simulated, derived, proxy or approximate?
    • Which approximation most needs replacing with a simulation?
    • After a geometry change, was the sweep re-run, or are old scores being quoted?
