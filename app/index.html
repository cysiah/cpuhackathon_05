Reading the starter critically what the organisers' sweep says when you check it (tools/critique_stats.py)

  • “1,041 designs pass” is mostly a darkening trick. Only 248 of them keep daylight ≥ 5% and 9 keep ≥ 10%. No passing design has an unshaded roof with g ≥ 0.5. EnergyPlus proxy for daylight
  • 800 of those 1,041 fail TM52 (the UK adaptive standard) when checked on the same hourly results. Only 241 pass both. A pass on the score is not a comfortable room. derived
  • The score is mostly radiant sun, not air. The baseline scores 321 K·h on operative temperature but 127 on air temperature; operative runs up to 5.9 K above air. One well-mixed zone cannot say where a person stands in that sun. This tool's map is built for exactly that gap. EnergyPlus
  • The street itself fails. Outdoor air scores 46.8 K·h against a 42 limit, so passing means the atrium must be cooler than outdoors at peak, which only night-cooled concrete or heavy shading can do.
  • Insulation looks harmful. Raising the glass U-value from 1.0 to 5.8 lowers the score by 233 K·h with no shading, but by only 6 behind a 90% screen. The lever rewards leaky glass in summer and the winter bill is outside the model proxy.
  • The surrogate is least trustworthy where it matters. Typical error is 2.2 K·h for designs scoring under 100, but the worst case is 20.4 K·h, half the pass limit. Near the pass line, check with a real run.
  • The map is an estimate on top of an average. EnergyPlus gives one temperature for the whole atrium. Our per-spot variation is the sun on a person (SolarCal) added to it and re-centred so the floor average matches EnergyPlus. It does not model stratification, wall surface temperatures, glare or wind.
  • The 2050 weather is a crude morph (+1.5 °C everywhere plus a 4 °C pulse). Use it to compare designs, not to predict a year.
